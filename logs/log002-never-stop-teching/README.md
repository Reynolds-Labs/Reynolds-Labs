## The Never-Ending Quest for Knowledge

I've always joked, **“I never stop teching.”**

Researching, experimenting, failing, succeeding, breaking things, fixing things, and everything in between. That's always been part of who I am.

I started Reynolds Labs a few months ago as a way to showcase my labs, experiments, and the work I put into what I like to call the never-ending quest for knowledge. The kind of learning that doesn't stop when the workday ends.

But my interest in technology started long before Reynolds Labs existed.

In fact, it started long before technology became my career.

I didn't go to college or officially enter the technology field until later in life, but I've always had my finger on the pulse of technology.

And looking back, I think I've spent most of my life finding excuses to experiment with it.

## Back When Technology Was a Little Different

I'm talking about connecting Xbox consoles with system-link cables before Xbox Live was even a thing.

Or getting my hands on **FruityLoops 2**, experimenting with music production and figuring out how to create my own instrumentals.

That's right. **FruityLoops 2.**

Now there's a throwback!

Over the years, I've dabbled in just about everything.

Video editing, music editing, photography, creating my own custom ringtones (remember when that was a thing?), and countless other projects that started with one simple thought:

**"I wonder if I can do that?"**

I remember when movie rental stores were awesome and Netflix would actually send DVDs through the mail.

Depending on your age, that might sound like ancient history!

But for me, those were some pretty exciting times to be exploring technology.

There was always something new to discover, something to experiment with, or some new piece of software that made me wonder what I could create.

And I usually couldn't resist finding out.

## Sometimes an Idea Becomes Something Bigger

At one point, armed with nothing more than a laptop and an idea, I created an entire murder mystery experience for more than 30 people, using technology to help bring the whole thing to life.

My wife and I even created a complete board game together, designing the board, cards, and everything that went along with it.

She's the artistic one in our little power team, and I'm usually the one figuring out how to make everything work.

Those projects weren't about building a résumé or demonstrating professional skills.

They were about taking an idea and seeing how far we could run with it.

And sometimes, that's where the most interesting experiences come from.

## Not Every Experiment Ends in Success

Of course, not every adventure in technology has been a success.

I've had plenty of projects that didn't work, ideas that sounded fantastic until I actually tried them, and those moments when you stare at something and think:

"Well, this just isn't going to happen."

Like the time a tiny rock got stuck in the trackball of my BlackBerry.

Naturally, I decided I could fix it myself.

Instead, I managed to launch that little navigation ball into another dimension.

To this day, I have absolutely no idea where it went.

But that's part of the fun, isn't it?

Sometimes you figure something out.

Sometimes you break something that was already broken.

And sometimes a perfectly good idea ends with you searching the floor for a tiny BlackBerry trackball that has apparently escaped the laws of physics.

Either way, you usually learn something.

## Making Something Out of What You Have

I've never considered myself some kind of tech master or wizard of the digital era.

I've just always been in the mix, diving into things, experimenting, seeing what I can figure out, and learning something along the way.

One thing I learned at a young age was how to take a big idea and make something out of it with very little in the way of resources.

Sometimes you don't have the newest equipment, the best software, or the perfect setup.

Sometimes all you have is curiosity, a little creativity, and the determination to see what happens.

And honestly, that's still how I approach a lot of things today.

I think there's something valuable about learning to work with what you have.

It encourages you to explore alternatives, think creatively, and occasionally discover solutions you might never have considered otherwise.

## Why Reynolds Labs Exists

Which brings me back to Reynolds Labs.

I started this portfolio because I wanted a place to document that curiosity and the work that comes from it.

Not just the finished projects, but the experiments, technical decisions, troubleshooting, successes, failures, and lessons learned along the way.

Right now, Reynolds Labs focuses primarily on my love for Linux, Linux administration, automation, and the freedom that I believe comes with an operating system that encourages you to explore, customize, build, and learn.

The portfolio currently features five completed technical labs covering Linux user provisioning, virtualization, automated backup and recovery, performance tuning, and private RPM repository administration.

I've also created **The Logs**, a place to share thoughts, experiences, software discoveries, and whatever else comes out of my adventures in technology.

Reynolds Labs is my independently developed personal and professional portfolio, separate from my employment.

It's simply a place to share what I'm building, what I'm learning, and the things that continue to spark my curiosity.

## Just the Beginning

I have plenty of ideas, projects, and experiments I'd like to explore.

Some will work.

Some probably won't.

And I'm sure a few will leave me staring at a screen wondering what on earth I just did.

But that's technology, and that's what keeps it interesting.

I don't know exactly where every idea will lead, and that's perfectly fine with me.

Sometimes the most interesting part of a project is discovering something you weren't even looking for.

So here's to curiosity, experimentation, and never running out of things to learn.

And as always...

**Never stop teching.**
